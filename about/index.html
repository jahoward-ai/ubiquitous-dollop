HCG helps Hamilton students understand consulting and prepare for recruiting. Quill gives selected participants practical experience through client work.

Mission & model

One organization, three connected pillars.

Industry Knowledge introduces students to consulting, firms, industries, and client work. Recruiting & Case Preparation builds practical readiness for applications and interviews. Quill is HCG’s student-operated applied consulting practice.

The Group teaches consulting; Quill practices consulting.

Membership

Different ways to participate.

General Membership

Open to interested Hamilton students, with no attendance obligation. Students can attend open programming without applying for leadership.

Join general programming

Active Membership

Earned by participating in two of three designated core events in a semester. The Group Managing Partner may recognize an equivalent contribution under the mandate.

Partnership and officers

Partners own portfolios and serve on the Executive Board alongside designated officers. These are leadership roles, not a prerequisite for general participation.

Partnership application

Quill

Project participation is selective and separate from membership or leadership status. No HCG title guarantees staffing on a client engagement.

How Quill participation works

Governance

Clear ownership, shared responsibility.

The Executive Board governs HCG. Partners lead defined portfolios, while officers support the Group’s operations, finances, and communications. Quill teams operate within HCG but use project-specific leadership, staffing, confidentiality, and professional standards.

Membership status, board leadership, and Quill project roles are related but distinct.

Why Hamilton

Hamilton’s liberal arts education builds habits students can apply in consulting.

Seminars train students to listen, ask better questions, and discuss competing interpretations. Writing-intensive courses demand clear arguments supported by evidence. Quantitative and qualitative work help students test a problem from more than one angle.

The open curriculum encourages students to connect ideas across disciplines. Campus leadership, athletics, clubs, and group projects add practice in ownership, teamwork, and direct communication.
