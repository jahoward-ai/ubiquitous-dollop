Recruiting

Work backward from the opportunity.

Firm research belongs in Industry Knowledge. This section focuses on the actions required to prepare and apply.

Timelines and networking

Track firm and program deadlines at their official sources, then give yourself time to learn about roles, offices, and practices. Use conversations to ask informed questions and test your understanding—not simply to request a referral.

Resumes and professional readiness

Show results, ownership, analytical work, collaboration, and communication clearly. Proofread carefully and be ready to explain every line. Hamilton’s Career Center can support broader application preparation.

Behavioral interviews

Prepare concise examples of leadership, problem solving, teamwork, setbacks, and judgment. Explain the situation, your specific actions, the result, and what you learned.

Case Preparation

Practice the thinking, not a script.

A case interview is a business problem discussed during an interview. It tests how you structure a question, use evidence, calculate, communicate, and synthesize.

Getting started

Learn the interview format and practice stating the objective in your own words.

Problem structuring

Break the question into useful parts and explain why the structure fits.

Quantitative practice

Build fluency with percentages, growth, weighted averages, breakeven, and clear mental math.

Exhibit interpretation

An exhibit is a chart or table. State what it shows, identify the important pattern, and connect it to the question.

Recommendations and synthesis

Answer the question directly, support the answer with evidence, note major risks, and name the next step.

Peer practice and feedback

Alternate interviewer and candidate roles, then give specific feedback on reasoning and communication.

A suggested sequence

Build the basics before adding speed.

  1. Understand the case-interview format.
  2. Practice structuring and basic calculations separately.
  3. Work through exhibits and short syntheses.
  4. Complete full cases with a peer.
  5. Review feedback, isolate weak skills, and repeat.
